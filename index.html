Computational Acoustic Ecology

Mapping soundscapes, ecological change, and linguistic diversity

The repositories within these pages contain data and tools created by researchers at IIT Guwahati working on mapping the soundscapes in various regions via computational acoustic ecology. The research is supported by the Mozilla Foundation through its Responsible Computing Challenge (RCC) program.

Soundscape refers to the composition of sounds heard in a natural or human-made environment. Linguistic elements are also part of the soundscape of an area or a community. The North-East of India is a particularly ecologically sensitive and linguistically diverse region. The region's environment and ecology are undergoing rapid changes due to a combination of urbanization, migration, and land-use change.

The markers for these changes can be observed via multiple sensing modalities, including images and soundscapes. Within the context of responsible computing, soundscapes are particularly beneficial for assessing ecological changes due to the low energy impact of collecting and transmitting sound data. Certain features are unique to sound, such as characteristic sounds of specific animals and human dialects. Anthropologists have also emphasized faithful documentation of soundscapes as a tool for investigating social and anthropological facets of human beings.

Research and Educational Goals

In this project, we utilize soundscapes from multiple urban and non-urban environments in North-East India and their analysis to create teaching resources for undergraduate education across several disciplines: humanities, design, computer science, and engineering.

The purpose of these pedagogical resources is to help introduce responsible computing in undergraduate teaching. This goal will be achieved by:

  1. showcasing the power of data and code to measure and assess changes in ecological and linguistic environments;
  2. raising awareness of the rich diversity of North-East India in ecological and linguistic dimensions; and
  3. encouraging undergraduate students to identify pathways for environmental stewardship through the use of computing technologies.
